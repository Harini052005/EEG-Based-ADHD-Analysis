Project Title: ADHD Analysis Using EEG: Testing Accuracy with Fewer Channels and Noise

Last Updated: October 2026

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. PROJECT OBJECTIVES & CORE RESEARCH QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project investigates two central research questions using machine learning
and signal processing:

  [Q1] Channel Reduction:
       Can machine learning models reliably detect ADHD patterns using a smaller,
       predefined subset of EEG channels (e.g., 4-5 frontal/central electrodes)
       compared to the full 19-channel clinical montage?

  [Q2] Noise Robustness:
       How does performance degrade when EEG signals are corrupted by controlled,
       simulated noise (e.g., environmental or movement artifacts)? Does the
       reduced-channel model suffer more or maintain comparable stability?

* IMPORTANT ETHICAL & SCIENTIFIC PRINCIPLE:
  This system is an academic research prototype and NOT a clinical diagnostic tool.
  Band powers and Theta/Beta ratios (TBR) are exploratory features, not diagnostic
  markers. Dataset labels are treated purely as research labels.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. DATASET SPECIFICATIONS (NASRABADI PEDIATRIC ADHD EEG DATASET)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The project utilizes the benchmark pediatric EEG dataset collected by researchers
at Shahed University (Ali Motie Nasrabadi et al.):

* Dataset Name: EEG Dataset for ADHD (61 ADHD and 60 Healthy Children)
* Official DOI: 10.21227/rzfh-zn36 (IEEE DataPort)
* Total Participants: 121 children
    - ADHD Group: 61 children (diagnosed by psychiatrist via DSM-IV criteria)
    - Control Group: 60 age-matched healthy children
* Participant Age Range: 7 to 12 years old (both boys and girls)
* Sampling Frequency: 128 Hz
* Reference Electrodes: Ear lobes (A1 and A2)
* Number of Channels: 19 scalp EEG channels
* Channel Layout: International Standard 10-20 Montage
    Frontal:   Fp1, Fp2, F7, F3, Fz, F4, F8
    Central:   C3, Cz, C4
    Temporal:  T3, T4, T5, T6
    Parietal:  P3, Pz, P4
    Occipital: O1, O2
* Recording Paradigm:
    Task-based recording during a visual attention task. Children were shown
    images containing a variable number of cartoon characters and asked to count
    them. The display duration depended on the child's response speed to sustain
    continuous cognitive engagement.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. FULL MONTAGE VS. CANDIDATE REDUCED MONTAGE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* Full Montage (19 Channels):
  All standard 10-20 channels:
  [Fp1, Fp2, F7, F3, Fz, F4, F8, T3, C3, Cz, C4, T4, T5, P3, Pz, P4, T6, O1, O2]

* Literature-Justified Reduced Montage Candidate (e.g., 4 to 5 Channels):
  Frontal and Central midline/bilateral sites:
  [Fz, Cz, F3, F4] (or optionally adding Pz)
  - Scientific Justification: Frontal-central regions reflect executive control,
    attention orienting, and working memory circuits commonly studied in ADHD
    literature.
  - RULE: Channel sets must be fixed BEFORE testing. Never pick channels based
    on test-set scores to prevent data snooping.

6. CRITICAL SCIENTIFIC RULES & ANTI-LEAKAGE PROTOCOL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Participant-Wise Splitting (Subject-Independent):
   - A random split of EEG segments mixes segments of the SAME child into both
     train and test sets. This causes severe data leakage and artificially high
     accuracy.
   - All epochs belonging to one child must remain strictly inside ONE fold
     (using StratifiedGroupKFold or GroupKFold).

2. Pipeline Integrity:
   - Feature scaling (StandardScaler), feature selection, and tuning must be
     fitted ONLY on training folds inside a scikit-learn Pipeline. Never fit on
     the whole dataset before splitting.

3. Honest Reporting:
   - Report Balanced Accuracy, Sensitivity, Specificity, F1-Score, and Confusion
     Matrices. If reduced channels perform worse or noise drops accuracy sharply,
     report it truthfully as a valid scientific finding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 3: Feature Extraction (Welch PSD: Theta 4-8Hz, Alpha 8-13Hz, Beta 13-30Hz, TBR)
Phase 4: Subject-Independent Cross-Validation setup (StratifiedGroupKFold)
Phase 5: Baseline Classifiers (Random Forest & SVM on full 19 channels)
Phase 6: Channel Reduction Experiment (Compare full 19 channels vs. 4-5 channels)
Phase 7: Noise Robustness Simulation (Add controlled noise levels to test sets)
Phase 8: Optional Extensions (Missing channel simulations & SHAP attribution stability)
Phase 9: Reproducibility Checks & Metrics Validation
